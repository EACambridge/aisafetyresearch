Privacy

How we use the details you give us through the forms on this site.

Who we are

This site is run by EA Cambridge, an unincorporated association based in Cambridge, United Kingdom, which is the controller of the personal data described here. In the rest of this notice, “we” and “us” mean the team that runs this site. This notice covers the sign-up form for finding a research team, the research submission form and accounts on this site; our full privacy policy explains your rights in more detail.

What we collect

When you sign up to find a research team, we collect your name, whether you’d prefer to be contacted by email or phone, your email address or phone number, your university email if you choose to give it, your AI safety experience, the role and research areas you’re interested in, the question you’d like to work on, whether you’d prefer to meet in person at your university, in person somewhere else or online, roughly how much time you have, when you’re usually free and whether we can show your name on the website.

When you submit research for review, we collect your name, your email address, the title, link and summary of your work, its research area and the names of any other authors you list.

If you create an account, we collect your email address and anything you add to your account: your name, whether we can show it on the website, your university, whether you want check-in emails, and the check-ins and write-ups you post. We also keep a short record of what you do in your account, such as when you signed up, updated your details or posted, so we can see how projects are going. We don’t record which pages you look at.

How we use it

We use your sign-up answers to match you with a research team and to support your team, for example by checking in on how your project is going. We rely on your consent, which you give by ticking the required box on the form.

If you also tick the optional box about recommendations, we may recommend you to organisations hiring for AI safety roles that we think are a good fit, and share your name, contact details and relevant answers with them. We’ll tell you which organisation and what we plan to share before we send anything, so you can say no. Once they have your details, their own privacy policy applies. You can sign up without ticking it.

The sign-up form also asks whether we can show your name on the website. If you agree, we show your full name or your first name, as you choose, next to the question your team is working on. If you choose to stay anonymous, we show you only as an anonymous member. We rely on your consent for this, and you can change your choice at any time by contacting us.

We use research submissions to review your work. If we accept it, we publish its title, summary, link and the authors’ names on this site. We rely on your consent, which you give by ticking the box on the submission form. If you list other authors, please check they’re happy to be named.

When we match you, we share your name and contact details with the people in your team, and they share theirs with you. If your project goes well, we may suggest opportunities such as mentored research programmes, but we will only pass your details to another organisation if you ask us to or tick the optional recommendations box.

We use your account to let you sign in, to show your team and posts, to send you check-in emails if you ask for them, and to review write-ups you submit. Committee members who run this project can see account details and posts so they can support teams. Check-ins stay private to you and us. We rely on your consent, which you give by creating an account, and you can withdraw it by deleting your account.

We do not sell your data or use it for advertising.

Where it is stored

Your answers go into Google Forms and Google Sheets held in our Google account. Only committee members who run this project can see them. Google may process the data outside the United Kingdom under a transfer arrangement recognised by UK data-protection law.

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you visit, GitHub receives technical information such as your internet protocol address and browser details, which it uses to deliver and secure the site. This site sets no cookies of its own and uses no analytics.

Accounts are run on Supabase, which stores account data on servers in Frankfurt, Germany. UK data-protection law recognises the European Union as protecting personal data adequately. When you sign in, your browser keeps a sign-in token so you stay signed in; it is needed for accounts to work and is removed when you sign out.

Checking your idea

When you use the Check your idea page, what you type is sent to Google’s Gemini, which turns it into search terms and compares it with papers found in public academic databases such as arXiv. The search terms, but not your full idea, are sent to those databases. We don’t store your idea or link it to you, and you don’t need to give your name.

We use Google’s free Gemini service, under which Google may use what you type to improve its products, so please don’t include anything confidential. A short-lived copy of the results is kept for up to six hours so that repeated checks of the same idea are faster.

How long we keep it

We keep your sign-up for 12 months after your last involvement with a project, then delete it. We keep research submissions we don’t publish for 12 months after we review them, then delete them. Published entries stay on the site until you ask us to remove them. We keep your account until you delete it; deleting it removes your profile, posts and activity record straight away. You can ask us to delete your data sooner at any time.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data, ask us to correct or delete it, or withdraw your consent at any time, including consent to being recommended to hiring organisations or to a published entry. Withdrawing consent stops future use but does not affect matching or recommendations we have already made. Contact jianxin@eacambridge.org.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.
