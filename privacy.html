Privacy

How we use the details you give us through the forms on this site.

Who we are

This site is run by EA Cambridge, an unincorporated association based in Cambridge, United Kingdom, which is the controller of the personal data described here. In the rest of this notice, “we” and “us” mean the team that runs this site. This notice covers signing up for a research team (which creates an account on this site), sharing completed projects, the forum and the check-in form; our full privacy policy explains your rights in more detail.

What we collect

When you sign up to find a research team, you create an account with your email address and then answer the sign-up questions. We collect your name, your university if you choose to give it, your AI safety experience, whether you’d rather work with people at a similar experience level, the role and research areas you’re interested in, the question you’d like to work on, whether you’d prefer to meet in person at your university, in person somewhere else or online, roughly how much time you have, when you’re usually free, whether we can show your name on the website and anything else you choose to tell us.

When you share a completed project, we collect its title, link and summary, the names of any other authors you list and, if you choose, which team it came from. When you post in the forum or comment on a project, we collect what you write.

Your account also holds the projects you share and your forum posts and comments. We also keep a short record of what you do in your account, such as when you signed up, updated your details or posted, so we can see how projects are going. We don’t record which pages you look at.

How we use it

We use your sign-up answers to match you with a research team and to support your team, for example by checking in on how your project is going. We rely on your consent, which you give by ticking the required box when you sign up.

After you’re matched, we send a short check-in each week and keep your answers, such as how your team is going and whether you’d like to be rematched. If we don’t hear from you for about three weeks, we mark you as inactive and leave you out of matching until you tell us you’d like to rejoin.

If you also tick the optional box about recommendations, we may recommend you to organisations hiring for AI safety roles that we think are a good fit, and share your name, contact details and relevant answers with them. Before we share anything, we’ll ask you separately for each organisation, saying which organisation it is and what we’d share, and we’ll only send your details if you say yes. Once they have your details, their own privacy policy applies. You can sign up without ticking it.

The sign-up also asks whether we can show your name on the website. If you agree, we show your full name or your first name, as you choose, next to the question your team is working on. If you choose to stay anonymous, we show you only as an anonymous member. We rely on your consent for this, and you can change your choice at any time in your account.

We read shared projects before listing them. If we list one, we publish its title, summary and link on the Completed projects page, with your name as you chose to show it on your account (full name, first name, or “A member”) and the other authors you named. If you list other authors, please check they’re happy to be named.

Forum threads, replies and comments on projects are public once an organiser has read and approved them. They show what you wrote and your name as you chose to show it on your account. You can delete your own posts and comments at any time, and an organiser can hide any that break the forum’s basic rules. We email you when your project is listed, when your thread is approved, and when someone’s comment on your project or thread is approved. We rely on your consent for all of this, which you give by sharing or posting.

When we match you, we share your name and contact details with the people in your team, and they share theirs with you. If your project goes well, we may suggest opportunities such as mentored research programmes, but we will only pass your details to another organisation if you ask us to or tick the optional recommendations box.

We use your account to let you sign in, to hold your sign-up answers, to show your team, and to let you share projects and post in the forum. Committee members who run this project can see account details, and read projects and forum posts before they appear, so they can support teams and keep the forum useful. Weekly check-ins stay private to you and us. We rely on your consent, which you give by creating an account, and you can withdraw it by deleting your account.

We do not sell your data or use it for advertising.

Where it is stored

Your sign-up answers are kept in your account and copied every hour to a Google Sheet held in our Google account, which we use to suggest teams. Check-in answers go into a Google Form and Google Sheet held in the same account. Shared projects and forum posts are stored with your account. Only committee members who run this project can see them. Google may process the data outside the United Kingdom under a transfer arrangement recognised by UK data-protection law.

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you visit, GitHub receives technical information such as your internet protocol address and browser details, which it uses to deliver and secure the site. This site sets no cookies of its own and uses no analytics.

Accounts are run on Supabase, which stores account data on servers in Frankfurt, Germany. UK data-protection law recognises the European Union as protecting personal data adequately. When you sign in, your browser keeps a sign-in token so you stay signed in; it is needed for accounts to work and is removed when you sign out. Sign-in emails are sent through Resend, an email delivery service, which receives your email address in order to send them.

Checking your idea

When you use the Check your idea page, what you type is sent to Google’s Gemini, which turns it into search terms and compares it with papers found in public academic databases such as arXiv. The search terms, but not your full idea, are sent to those databases. We don’t store your idea or link it to you, and you don’t need to give your name.

We use Google’s free Gemini service, under which Google may use what you type to improve its products, so please don’t include anything confidential. A short-lived copy of the results is kept for up to six hours so that repeated checks of the same idea are faster.

How long we keep it

We keep your sign-up for 12 months after your last involvement with a project, then delete it. Listed projects and approved forum posts stay on the site until you delete them, delete your account, or ask us to remove them. We keep your account until you delete it; deleting it removes your profile, posts and activity record straight away. You can ask us to delete your data sooner at any time.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data, ask us to correct or delete it, or withdraw your consent at any time, including consent to being recommended to hiring organisations or to a published entry. Withdrawing consent stops future use but does not affect matching or recommendations we have already made. Contact jianxin@eacambridge.org.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.
