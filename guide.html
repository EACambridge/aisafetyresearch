How to run your project

Five steps that make the difference between a project that fizzles out and one that finishes.

  1. Research how to do your research

    We recommend you make this your first step, and spend a week or two on it before running anything.

    Find out how people already tackle questions like yours. It shows you where your project can add something new, gives you a plan you can defend, and is exactly what funders look for when you apply. It is also what makes the research useful rather than a repeat of something already done.

    • Read the closest existing work. Check your idea finds the papers nearest to yours.
    • Learn the methods those papers use. ARENA teaches the technical tools, and BlueDot Impact runs free courses on AI safety and governance.
    • New to the field? Start with our learning resources.
    • Talk to someone who has done similar work. A short message to a paper’s author, or a post on the Alignment Forum, often saves weeks.

  2. Write a one-page plan

    Your question, why it matters, how you’ll answer it, what you’ll have by the end of term, and what you need: money, compute or a mentor. Agree it as a team. You’ll reuse it for every funding application.

  3. Find funding

    Small, specific requests, such as compute for one experiment, are usually the quickest to get. Send your one-page plan with a clear budget.

    • BlueDot Impact Rapid Grants: small, fast grants for projects like yours.
    • Manifund: post your project publicly and funders can back it.
    • Transformative AI Fund (EA Funds): grants to individuals and new projects, applications open all year.
    • AISafety.com funding directory and Manifund’s AI safety funder bulletin: up-to-date lists of who funds what.

    Funders change often. Check each one is still open before you apply.

  4. Meet every week

    Pick a fixed time, keep short notes of what you tried and what’s next, and answer our one-minute check-in. If you’re stuck or the team isn’t working, say so in the check-in and we’ll help.

  5. Share what you found

    Write it up, even if the result wasn’t what you hoped; a clear negative result saves others time. Share it from your account and we’ll list it on Completed projects, where people can read it and comment.
