Helpful guide to start your project

A rough outline, not a rulebook. Work out how your team works best and get going.

  1. Find the most impactful version of your research

    Before you dive in, ask which version of your question would matter most if you answered it. Then build a theory of change for it: the chain from what you’ll do to the difference it makes, with the reason each step leads to the next.

    There are plenty of ways to do this, so pick whatever suits your team. As a rough idea, it might look like this:

    Run the experiment → publish what we find → safety researchers pick up the method → hidden failures get caught before models are released.

  2. Apply for funding

    A clear theory of change is most of a good application. AISafety.com keeps a directory of funders for AI safety work, so you can find grants and compute for your project.

    See funding sources on AISafety.com

  3. If you’re rejected, find out why

    Ask for feedback and work out what went wrong. If your question is a good one, you really shouldn’t be rejected, so the reason tells you what to change.

  4. Do the research anyway

    Funding or not, get going. Ask the forum when you want feedback, and share what you find on Completed projects when you’re done.
